IMAGE SETTINGS

Why these sizes and backgrounds?

Resolution

The synthetic config uses 256 × 256 pixels. The real config resizes each image to a 390-pixel long edge and derives the short edge from its camera aspect ratio. These are practical experiment budgets, not resolutions selected by a quality sweep; the repository does not record why the exact values were chosen.

Synthetic white

NeRF-synthetic images have transparency. The training loader composites their RGBA pixels over white, and the renderer uses white for any remaining transmittance. This gives reference and render the same background.

Real black

Real photographs are RGB and keep their photographed backgrounds. Black is the renderer's fallback color where splats leave transmittance; it does not replace the photograph's background. All three kernels use the same choice, which can matter in under-covered regions.

A compute-budget rationale for the image sizes is an inference from the configs, not a documented optimization study. Changing resolution or background would be a different experiment.

MATCHED WORKFLOW

From photographs to a paired result

  1. Prepare a fixed split

    Keep training, validation, and held-out test cameras separate. Synthetic scenes reuse one generated point initialization; real scenes reuse the same COLMAP points within each scene.

  2. Train matched runs

    Run Gaussian, linear radial, and polynomial kernels with seeds 7, 17, and 27. Within a scene, keep resolution, optimizer, loss, densification, splat cap, camera split, and initialization matched.

  3. Evaluate the fixed final checkpoint

    Render held-out test views after 8,000 synthetic or 20,000 real-scene steps. Validation curves and saved snapshots diagnose training behavior; they do not select the primary test checkpoint.

  4. Compare within scenes

    Pair each alternative with Gaussian using the same scene and seed. Report per-scene differences before discussing patterns across scenes; individual camera views are not independent scenes.

MEASUREMENTS

What the image scores capture

PSNR ↑

Peak signal-to-noise ratio converts average squared pixel error to decibels. Higher means closer pixel values; small shifts or blur can affect it.

SSIM ↑

Structural similarity compares local brightness, contrast, and spatial structure. Higher means more similar local image patterns.

LPIPS ↓

Learned perceptual image patch similarity compares image features from a pretrained network; this project uses VGG. Lower means closer features.

Visual checks

Matched rendered crops can show visible edge, texture, blur, and artifact differences that a single score may miss.

Training behavior

Validation curves, saved checkpoints, splat count, and final-versus-best validation scores reveal convergence and late declines.

Rendering cost

Track render time, training time, tile pairs, and sampled peak GPU memory. Compare elapsed times only on matched GPU models; tile pairs measure work, not wall-clock time.

The two matrices differ in scene type, resolution, background, initialization, capacity, training schedule, and radius guard. Cross-matrix differences therefore describe patterns; they do not isolate a single cause.
